How to Fund an Escrow Payment From Your Crypto Wallet

Fund an escrow payment by matching the asset and network in the instructions, checking the deposit address, and sending only the stated amount and fee.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 17, 2026 · 03:06 UTC·3 min read

To fund an escrow payment from a crypto wallet, send the agreed asset on the specified network to the deposit address or contract named in the escrow instructions. Confirm the amount, fee and release conditions before signing; a transaction sent to the wrong address or chain may not be recoverable.

What should you check before sending?

Check that the escrow instructions identify the asset, network, exact amount, deposit address and any required memo or reference. A token with the same name can exist on several networks, and an address that looks familiar does not prove it is the right destination.

If the escrow accepts funds on a different chain, the route adds another step; this guide to mantle bridge compares three routes and their trade-offs. Use a bridge only if the escrow instructions require it, and verify that the resulting token and network match what the escrow accepts.

How do you send the payment from your wallet?

Open the wallet that holds the agreed asset, select its matching network, and enter the escrow address exactly as provided. Review the wallet’s transaction summary before approving it: check the destination, token, amount and network fee. Keep enough of the network’s native token to pay that fee; the escrowed asset may not cover it.

  • Copy the address from the escrow instructions and check its beginning and end after pasting.
  • Enter any required memo or reference exactly as shown.
  • Compare the final amount and network with the escrow terms before signing.
  • Save the transaction ID so you can check its status or share it with the escrow service.

If the service supplies a payment page or wallet connection, confirm you are using the intended service before connecting or signing. A wallet signature can authorize more than a simple transfer, so read the prompt and reject approvals you do not understand.

When is the escrow payment funded?

A wallet showing “sent” means the transaction was submitted; it does not by itself confirm that the escrow has credited the payment. Check the transaction on the relevant network and follow the escrow service’s stated confirmation or deposit-status process.

Funding and release are separate steps. The escrow terms should say what event releases the funds to the recipient and what process applies if the parties disagree or the transaction is cancelled. Keep those terms and the transaction record until the payment is resolved.

The practical rule is to follow the escrow’s asset and network instructions exactly, then verify the deposit before treating the payment as funded. The escrow’s status and release conditions determine what happens next; if either is unclear, ask the service before sending.
