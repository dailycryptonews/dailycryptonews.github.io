Paying an onchain freelancer from Monero

Paying an onchain freelancer from Monero means converting XMR into the asset and network in the invoice, then checking delivery and documenting the swap.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 3, 2026 · 15:10 UTC·2 min read

To pay an onchain freelancer from Monero, convert XMR into the token and network named on the invoice, then send that asset to the freelancer’s address. Monero cannot be sent directly to an address on another blockchain; the conversion route is a separate step with its own fees, timing and trust assumptions.

Ask the freelancer to specify the token, network, exact address and amount due, including whether they expect a particular stablecoin or native asset. For background on the available routes, read this guide to choosing an XMR bridge route; the right option depends on what asset must arrive and what conversion services you can use.

What should the invoice specify?

An invoice should name the asset and network separately: the same token name can exist on multiple chains, and an address for one chain may not accept deposits on another. Confirm the amount, deadline and who covers conversion and network fees before you quote the XMR needed.

Get the destination address from the freelancer directly and confirm it through a second channel if the invoice arrived by email or chat. A copied address can be replaced, and a transaction sent to the wrong network or address may be difficult or impossible to recover.

How do you convert XMR into the payment asset?

Choose a conversion path that delivers the exact token on the exact network in the invoice. A route may use an exchange or swap service, or more than one conversion step; check what asset you must deposit, what asset you will receive, the quoted rate, fees, limits and expected processing time before sending.

  • Compare the final amount delivered, after service and network fees, with the invoice.
  • Check whether the service requires an account or identity checks before you transfer funds.
  • Confirm the destination network and address format on the receiving side.
  • Save the quote and transaction records for your invoice and accounting.

Do not treat the quoted XMR amount as fixed if the conversion rate can move before execution. Agree with the freelancer whether they invoice a fixed token amount or accept the amount that a stated XMR budget buys.

How can you confirm the freelancer was paid?

After the conversion completes, send the received asset to the freelancer’s verified address on the agreed network. Share the destination-chain transaction ID and wait for the freelancer to confirm receipt; the transaction ID can help both parties check the transfer, but it does not by itself prove the person controlling the address is the freelancer.

Monero transaction details are private on the public blockchain, so the XMR leg does not provide the same public payment record as the destination-chain transfer. If you need to prove the Monero payment to a conversion provider or another party, Monero wallets can generate transaction proofs; share only the proof details needed for that purpose.

The practical choice is to treat conversion and payment as two linked transfers: confirm the invoice first, verify the route and destination, then keep records for both legs. The freelancer’s receipt and any service processing time remain to be confirmed after you send; a bridge route does not guarantee a specific delivery time or final amount.
