Four Checks Before You Mark a Cross-Chain Transfer Complete

A cross-chain transfer is done only when the destination chain records the expected asset at the intended address; check the route, receipt, amount and fees.

The Blockheight Editors·Sep 29, 2026·2 min read

A cross-chain transfer is done when the destination chain credits the expected asset to the intended address, not when the source wallet says “sent.” Check the route’s status and the destination transaction before treating the funds as available. A transfer may involve several on-chain steps, so a successful source transaction alone does not prove delivery.

What should I check before sending a cross-chain transfer?

Check the destination chain, receiving address, token and quoted amount before approving the source transaction. A correct address on the wrong network may not receive the asset you expect, and token names can refer to different contracts on different chains.

Compare the quote with what you intend to receive, including any displayed fees and minimum output. A routed swap can include a trade before or after the bridge, so the destination token may differ from the one sent. For a Rango route, the rango bridge walkthrough covers the steps from start to finish.

Which four checks show whether the transfer is done?

Use four checks: confirm the source transaction, follow the route, verify destination delivery and compare the credited amount. A route tracker can connect these steps, but the destination chain’s own record is the evidence that the asset arrived.

  • Source transaction: Find the transaction hash in your wallet or the source-chain explorer. Confirm it succeeded and wait for the network’s required confirmations; a pending or failed transaction has not completed the first leg.
  • Route status: Check the transfer’s progress in the interface that created it. A cross-chain swap may involve a source-chain action, a bridge transfer and a destination-chain swap, so one hash may not describe the whole route.
  • Destination receipt: Verify a successful transaction or delivery record on the destination chain. Match the receiving address and token contract, rather than relying only on a wallet notification or a balance display.
  • Amount and availability: Compare the credited amount with the quote, accounting for displayed fees and price movement. Confirm the asset is spendable in the intended wallet; a token may be present on-chain but absent from the wallet’s default token list.

These checks separate a delayed transfer from a completed one. If the source transaction succeeded but the route remains pending, use the route’s status details and transaction hashes to identify which step is outstanding; avoid starting a second transfer just because the first is taking longer than expected.

What if the destination asset has not appeared?

First check the destination explorer for the receiving address and the expected token. If the record shows delivery but the wallet balance does not, check that the wallet is viewing the correct network and token contract. If the destination transaction failed or the route tracker reports an error, follow the route’s stated recovery process and keep the transaction hashes for support.

Do not call a transfer complete based only on a source-side success message or an estimated arrival time. Wait for destination-side evidence that the expected asset reached the intended address; if the route is still pending, its final status remains unconfirmed.
