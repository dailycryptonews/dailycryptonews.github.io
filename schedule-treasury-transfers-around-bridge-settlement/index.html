Schedule treasury transfers around bridge settlement

Schedule crypto treasury transfers for when funds must arrive, then reconcile bridge settlement separately; delivery and final reimbursement can follow different clocks.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 15, 2026 · 20:51 UTC·3 min read

Treasury teams should schedule crypto bridge transfers for the time funds must arrive on the destination chain, while tracking settlement as a separate accounting event. A transfer can become spendable before the bridge finishes verifying and reimbursing the liquidity provider or relayer that advanced the funds.

Why can bridge delivery and settlement happen at different times?

Many bridges use relayers or liquidity providers to deliver funds before the underlying transfer is fully settled. The recipient sees the destination-chain transfer, while the bridge later checks deposits and fills, then processes reimbursements and liquidity movements.

Across, for example, has relayers front destination-chain funds and seek reimbursement through settlement bundles after verification. For the user-side steps, see this Acrossbridge guide to moving or swapping crypto. The operational point is to distinguish “received” from “settled” in treasury records.

When should a treasury initiate a bridge transfer?

Initiate it early enough to absorb the route’s variable delivery time, and set the deadline from the business need rather than an assumed bridge average. Check the bridge’s current quote, estimated arrival, route, token and destination network before signing; the estimate is a planning input, not a settlement guarantee.

Use a liquidity buffer when the destination funds must support payroll, exchange collateral or a scheduled payment. Keep that buffer on the destination chain if a missed deadline would be more costly than holding idle funds there.

How should teams reconcile transfers after delivery?

Maintain one record per transfer that connects the treasury instruction, source transaction and destination receipt. Record the received amount separately from fees and from any later settlement or reimbursement status reported by the bridge.

  • Confirm the destination network and token contract match the payment instruction.
  • Set an arrival deadline and an escalation contact for delayed transfers.
  • Require a second review for address changes or unusually large amounts.
  • Reconcile destination receipts against source debits and bridge fees.

Do not count an expected transfer as destination liquidity until the funds are visible and usable there. Settlement status matters for audit and exposure tracking, but it does not replace confirmation that the recipient can spend the funds.

What should treasury teams monitor next?

Track delivery time and final settlement time as separate measures for each route, then adjust cutoffs and buffers when actual performance changes. The better default for most teams is to plan against the slower operational deadline and release excess buffer only after destination receipt is confirmed.

Bridge designs and route conditions differ, so the exact delay and settlement process remain specific to the selected bridge. Before each transfer, verify the live route estimate and confirm whether the bridge has reported the transfer as delivered and settled.
