Bungee Bridge routes: compare fees, slippage and output

Compare Bungee Bridge routes by the tokens delivered after fees, the minimum output allowed by slippage, network gas and timing—not by the lowest headline fee.

The Blockheight Editors·Sep 28, 2026·3 min read

Choose a bungee bridge route by comparing the destination amount after fees, the minimum output allowed by slippage, and the gas and time needed to execute—not by the fee line alone. The route with the lowest stated fee can still leave you with fewer tokens if its swap has a worse rate or higher price impact.

What should you compare in a bungee bridge quote?

Compare the same transfer amount, source and destination chains, and destination token across every route. Bungee’s quote fields include expected output, minimum output, gas cost, suggested slippage and estimated time, which lets you compare the full transaction rather than a single fee figure.

Read the expected output as the estimated amount delivered if the quoted route executes at its quoted terms. The minimum output is the floor permitted by the slippage setting; it is not a promise that the route will deliver the estimate. When reading a Bungee Bridge route quote, check which token the destination wallet will receive and whether the displayed gas figure covers only the source transaction or another step too.

How do fees and slippage change the final amount?

Fees pay for parts of the transfer, while slippage describes how much the swap’s execution price may move against the quote before the transaction is rejected. A route can include a bridge fee, swap costs and network gas; where those costs appear depends on the route and how the quote presents them.

Swap price impact also matters: a large trade against a shallow liquidity pool can get a worse rate than a small one. That difference may be reflected in the quoted output before execution, while slippage tolerance sets how much further the output may worsen and still be accepted. A bungee bridge route with a broad tolerance may be more likely to execute through price movement, but it can also allow a less favourable minimum.

When is the cheapest route the better choice?

Pick the route with the best net result for your purpose, not automatically the smallest fee. For a fixed input, compare the destination value after costs; for a fixed target amount, compare how much you must send, the required gas and the route’s minimum-output conditions.

  • Check the source and destination networks and confirm the exact token on each.
  • Compare expected output and minimum output for the same input amount.
  • Include gas in the sending network’s native token, especially if your wallet balance is low.
  • Use estimated time as a trade-off: a cheaper route may take longer, depending on its transfer mechanism.

For most readers, a route with a competitive net output and a sensible minimum is preferable to one that wins only on its fee label. If two routes are close, favour the clearer output and tighter execution floor over a small quoted saving that depends on accepting a much worse rate.

What should you check before sending?

Refresh the quote just before signing because fees, gas prices and available liquidity can change. Confirm the recipient, networks, input amount, output token and minimum received; then check that the transaction shown by your wallet matches the route you selected.

After signing, the transfer still has to complete its source-chain transaction and destination-side steps, which can affect timing. The quote provides an estimate, not confirmation of final delivery; the final amount and completion time remain unconfirmed until the route executes.
