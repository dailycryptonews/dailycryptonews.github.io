Fairshake backs 32 House candidates with six $1 million pledges

Fairshake named 32 House candidates for midterm support, including six $1 million commitments, as Senate crypto rules remain stalled after September's failed vote.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 11, 2026 · 01:19 UTC·2 min read

Fairshake said Monday, Oct. 5, that it will support 32 House candidates in November’s midterms, including six candidates set to receive $1 million each, according to The Associated Press’s report. The slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats; all 32 backed legislation that was a priority for the crypto industry.

How much is Fairshake committing to House races?

Fairshake disclosed $1 million in spending for each of three Democrats — Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California — and three Republicans: Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. The group did not disclose spending for the other 26 candidates, so the total House commitment is unknown.

Fairshake and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash at the end of August. The group has not said whether it will spend more in House or Senate contests before Election Day.

Which stalled bill links the slate to crypto policy?

The candidates backed the CLARITY Act, which would establish a federal regulatory framework for digital assets. The Senate failed to advance the legislation in September after opponents said it did not adequately address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments, according to AP’s report on the Senate vote.

Fairshake spokesperson Geoff Vetter said the group backs candidates in both parties who support crypto. He said Fairshake and its affiliates spent $40 million supporting 29 Democrats and $31 million supporting 28 Republicans in primaries and special elections this year.

Will Fairshake spend more before November?

The group has already announced a separate plan to spend nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. Its next House or Senate spending decisions remain unannounced; the November midterms will determine which candidates win seats, while the timing and prospects for renewed action on the CLARITY Act remain uncertain.
