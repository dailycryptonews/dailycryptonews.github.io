Fairshake names 32 House candidates for midterm support

Fairshake named 32 House candidates for November support, with $1 million each for six, as the stalled CLARITY Act leaves crypto rules unresolved.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 9, 2026 · 16:20 UTC·2 min read

Fairshake said Monday, Oct. 5, it will support 32 House candidates in November’s midterms, including six slated for $1 million each, as the crypto industry looks to build support for legislation that stalled in the Senate, according to the Associated Press report. The slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, all of whom supported legislation that was a top industry priority.

Which candidates will receive the $1 million commitments?

Fairshake named three Democrats — Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California — and three Republicans — Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan — for $1 million in spending each. The group did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates, the AP reported.

The planned spending is campaign support from a political action committee. Roll Call’s account of the announcement says Fairshake’s broader list includes House incumbents from both parties and that the committee did not specify the amounts for the other races.

What legislation stalled in the Senate?

The CLARITY Act would establish rules for digital-asset markets. The House passed it in July 2025, but the Senate blocked it last month after lawmakers failed to agree on ethics guardrails, among other issues, according to Roll Call. The AP reported that opponents said the bill did not adequately address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments.

Fairshake spokesperson Geoff Vetter said the group backs “pro-crypto candidates” in both parties. The AP reported that Fairshake and affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash at the end of August, and that the group did not say whether it would spend more in House or Senate races before November.

What remains unknown before the midterms?

The six $1 million commitments are the only amounts disclosed for the House slate. The group has announced plans to spend nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio, but its total spending across the 32 House races and any further Senate commitments remain unconfirmed.
