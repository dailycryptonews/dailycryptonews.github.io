What to do when a bridge session expires before confirmation

A bridge page timing out does not prove a transfer failed; check the source transaction, route status and recovery conditions before retrying the transfer.

The Blockheight Editors·Sep 29, 2026·2 min read

A bridge session can expire before confirmation when its page or wallet connection times out while a transaction is still pending. That timeout alone does not show whether funds moved: the source-chain transaction and the bridge’s route status determine what happened.

Bridge interfaces can differ in how they handle expired sessions, so check the route’s steps before acting. For a closer look at how route choices affect a transfer, see this guide to choosing a Mantle Bridge transfer path.

What expires when a bridge session expires?

Usually, the browser page, wallet connection or quoted route expires; the submitted blockchain transaction has its own status. A wallet may stop showing a pending prompt after a refresh, but that does not cancel a transaction already broadcast to the network.

Bridges move assets through different mechanisms. A route may lock tokens on one chain and release them on another, or burn tokens on the source chain and mint them on the destination. The interface timeout is separate from those on-chain actions, and the exact recovery options depend on the route.

How can you tell whether the transfer went through?

Start with the source-chain transaction hash, if the wallet provided one. An explorer can show whether that transaction is pending, confirmed or failed; then check the bridge’s route status to see whether its next step has completed.

A confirmed source transaction does not always mean the destination transfer is complete. The bridge may still need to process a message or wait for another step, while a failed source transaction generally means that the intended transfer did not begin. Check the destination address and token balance as well, since a completed transfer may not be visible in the original bridge tab.

What should you check before retrying?

Match the transfer details against the route’s status before submitting anything again. These checks help distinguish a lost display from a failed transaction:

  • Find the source transaction hash in the wallet’s activity or the bridge’s history.
  • Confirm the source and destination networks, token and recipient address.
  • Check whether the route offers a resume, claim or refund step, and follow its stated conditions.
  • Compare the new quote with the original amount and fees before approving another transaction.

Do not assume that reopening the page or repeating the approval will resume the original route. A second transaction can create a separate transfer and may require another fee; if the first transaction is confirmed but the route remains unresolved, use the bridge’s documented recovery process.

Which recovery path should you use?

If the source transaction failed, verify the reason before trying again. If it confirmed, wait for the route’s next step or use its listed recovery action; a refund may not be available until that route’s conditions are met.

The practical rule is to treat the chain record as the evidence and the page timeout as a display or session problem until checked. What happens next depends on the source transaction and route status; whether a particular expired session can be resumed or refunded remains specific to that bridge and transfer.
