Solana moves Alpenglow upgrade to public testnet

Solana has moved Alpenglow to its public testnet, where validators are testing a consensus change targeting finality in about 150 milliseconds, down from 12.8 seconds.

Hashmark NewsroomSep 24, 20262 min read

Solana has moved its Alpenglow consensus upgrade to the public testnet, beginning broader testing of a change that targets transaction finality in about 150 milliseconds instead of 12.8 seconds, CoinDesk reported on September 23. The Solana Foundation’s Alpenglow upgrade page now lists testnet activation as active and devnet and mainnet as not activated.

What changes in Solana’s consensus system?

Alpenglow replaces TowerBFT, Solana’s current system for validators to agree on transaction order and finality, according to the Solana Foundation. Under TowerBFT, validators record votes as transactions on the chain; a block becomes final after enough votes accumulate across 32 slots.

Alpenglow’s first phase, Votor, has validators send votes directly to one another and combine them into certificates. The Foundation says a block can become final after one or two rounds of voting. Its estimate of roughly 150 milliseconds is a target, not a guaranteed constant: the Foundation says timing depends on the stake distribution and whether finality takes one round or two.

What does the testnet move mean for users and operators?

The public test network lets developers and validators test the migration without risking users’ funds, CoinDesk reported. The upgrade changes how validators reach consensus, while the Foundation says transaction execution, programs, transaction formats and fees stay the same.

Validators participating in the initial migration need Agave 4.3, Anza’s validator software, CoinDesk reported. The outlet said alternative validator clients Firedancer and Frankendancer do not yet support the test, so the first migration will run through Agave.

The testnet step also gives infrastructure providers a chance to check how the new finality process affects their services. CoinDesk reported that exchanges rely on finality before crediting deposits, bridges before releasing funds on another blockchain, and merchants before treating payments as irreversible. The Foundation says vote transactions will leave blocks under Votor, and that developers who stream block data will need to account for changes to that data.

When could Alpenglow reach Solana’s main network?

It has not activated on mainnet, according to the Foundation’s current upgrade status. CoinDesk reported that September 28 appeared in Anza’s schedule as a tentative date to resume feature activation on mainnet; the outlet said it was not a confirmed Alpenglow launch date.

The Foundation lists Votor as the first phase and Rotor, a planned replacement for Solana’s block data propagation system, as a later release once Votor is adopted. Its page says Rotor is not yet scheduled.
