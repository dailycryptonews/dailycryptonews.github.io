Revoking a token approval can interrupt recurring swaps

Revoking a token allowance can make the next scheduled swap fail, but it may not cancel the schedule; check the spender, balance and automation status.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 16, 2026 · 12:34 UTC·3 min read

Revoking a token approval can stop a recurring swap when its next execution needs to spend that token. The schedule may remain active, because the allowance and the automation that triggers swaps are separate permissions.

Under the ERC-20 token standard, an allowance lets a specified spender transfer up to an approved amount of a token from your wallet. A swap service may use a router or another contract as that spender. If the allowance is revoked or runs out, a later swap that needs the token can fail; the precise result depends on how the service handles failed executions.

If your setup also involves moving bitcoin to Ethereum, read this guide to bridging Bitcoin to Ethereum with QuickSwap. Bridging and swap approvals are separate steps, so completing one does not necessarily grant permission for the other.

Does revoking approval cancel the recurring schedule?

No: revoking a token allowance removes permission to spend, but does not necessarily delete a schedule held by a separate service or contract. The schedule might keep attempting to run, pause after a failure, or require you to cancel it manually.

Check the service’s status page or transaction history after revocation. Look for a failed execution, a paused schedule, or a control to cancel future runs. If the service charges fees on failed attempts, its terms should say when those charges apply.

What should you check before the next swap?

Check which token and spender the scheduled swap needs, then compare the allowance with the amount the swap may use. The allowance is permission, not a deposit: tokens remain in your wallet unless a transaction transfers them.

  • Confirm the scheduled pair and input token.
  • Check whether the spender address matches the service’s current instructions.
  • Review the allowance and wallet balance for the token being spent.
  • Check whether the schedule is active, paused or cancelled.

Some approvals set a high or unlimited allowance, while others authorize a limited amount. A limited allowance can be used up by successful swaps; a revoked allowance cannot authorize a new transfer until you approve the spender again. Approve only the amount and spender needed for the schedule.

How do you resume recurring swaps safely?

First decide whether you want the schedule to run again. If not, cancel it through the service that created it; revocation alone may leave it queued. If you do want it to continue, verify the spender and schedule settings, then submit a new allowance transaction for the required token.

Wait for the approval transaction to confirm before relying on the next run. A successful approval restores permission, but it does not guarantee execution: the schedule still needs to be active, and the wallet must meet the service’s balance and network requirements. The next attempt’s status will show whether the swap resumed; whether failed runs are retried depends on the service.
