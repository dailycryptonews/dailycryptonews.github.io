Why refresh token metadata after a cross-chain transfer?

Refreshing token metadata after a bridge can correct a wallet’s symbol, decimals, logo or token listing, but it does not alter the asset or finish a transfer.

The Blockheight Editors·Sep 29, 2026·2 min read

A cross-chain transfer can leave a wallet showing stale token details because the destination token may have a different address or may not be in the wallet’s cache. Refreshing metadata makes the wallet look up the destination token again; it does not move funds or change the token contract.

Why can token details change after a bridge?

A bridge may release an existing asset on the destination chain or issue a representation of it, depending on how that bridge works. The destination token can have its own contract address and metadata, even when it represents the same underlying asset.

Wallets and portfolio apps often cache token names, symbols and logos so they do not have to fetch them repeatedly. After a transfer, that cache may still show the source-chain token, omit the destination token, or display a generic label. A guide to estimating source-chain gas for a Bungee Bridge transfer covers a separate part of the process: the fee needed to start a transfer.

What does refreshing metadata actually update?

A refresh asks the wallet or data provider to fetch token details again, usually using the destination chain and token contract address. Depending on the app, that can update the displayed name, symbol, decimals, logo, or whether the token appears in the asset list.

Those details help the app display a balance in human-readable form. The token contract holds the balance; the wallet reads it and formats it using token information. Metadata refresh can fix a display problem, but it cannot repair a failed transfer, recover funds sent to the wrong address, or make an unsupported token transferable.

For most users, the key check is the destination chain and contract address. A familiar symbol is not enough to identify a token: different contracts can use the same symbol, and a bridged representation may not share the source token’s address.

When should you refresh, and what should you check?

Refresh after the bridge reports completion on the destination chain, especially if the received token is missing or its displayed details look wrong. If the transfer is still pending, the destination balance may not exist yet for the wallet to show.

  • Confirm the destination network shown in the wallet.
  • Check the destination token address against the bridge’s transfer details or another trusted source.
  • Refresh the asset list or token data; if needed, add the token using its verified contract address.
  • Compare the displayed balance with the destination-chain activity before taking further action.

Decimals affect how a raw token balance is formatted, so a wrong display can make a balance look unexpectedly large or small. Do not send or swap based only on a ticker or logo; verify the chain and contract first. Refreshing metadata is a reasonable first step for a missing or mislabelled asset, but it is not proof that the transfer settled.

Once the destination transaction is confirmed and the wallet has refreshed its token data, the asset should appear with the information the app can retrieve. Whether a particular wallet supports that token, and whether the bridge transfer has fully completed, must still be confirmed in the app and on the destination chain.
