US wins authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 17, 2026 · 12:43 UTC·2 min read

The US Justice Department said Nov. 12 it had obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The department’s announcement said the amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through government and private-sector efforts.

How much was stolen, and what has been frozen?

The department said members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The Justice Department did not say the full $1.2 billion had been transferred to government control: it said a court had authorized the US to seize or restrain that amount from the larger frozen total.

The Associated Press reported that the FBI had attributed the theft to TraderTraitor. The AP’s report on the seizure said the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Who is working to recover the assets?

The Justice Department said its National Security and Criminal divisions, the US Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia and law enforcement partners have worked with virtual asset service providers to freeze and trace the stolen funds. The FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit are coordinating recovery efforts with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation.

US officials say the actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The FBI has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving wallet addresses tied to the theft.

What happens next?

Investigators are continuing to trace, freeze, seize and seek forfeiture of the assets, according to the Justice Department. The department has not confirmed how much of the court-authorized amount will ultimately be forfeited or returned to Binance or other claimants; that determination depends on further proceedings.
