DOJ seeks forfeiture of $1.17B in USDC tied to Binance hack

The Justice Department seeks forfeiture of more than $1.1 billion in USDC linked to the Binance hack, with Circle offering replacement tokens if a court orders it.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 24, 2026 · 06:21 UTC·2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department filed a civil forfeiture complaint on Nov. 23 seeking more than $1.1 billion in cryptocurrency linked to the Oct. 29 Binance hack; balances in the filing total about $1.17 billion, according to The Block’s report on the filing. The DOJ says the funds were criminally obtained by North Korean actors and the case is part of an effort to help Binance recover stolen assets.

The complaint covers USDC associated with 36 addresses across 18 blockchains. The DOJ identifies the suspected actors as Lazarus Group/APT38. The filing describes a hack discovered by Binance employees on Oct. 29, when attackers gained unauthorized access to the exchange’s network and hot wallets.

The complaint says Circle blocked access to the USDC on Oct. 29, preventing transfers from the listed addresses. But those addresses remain under the attackers’ control, and the government says it cannot access the tokens directly.

Circle told the government it cannot transfer the original USDC from the blocked addresses. Instead, it agreed to issue an equivalent amount of new USDC and transfer it to the United States if the court grants a forfeiture order, according to the DOJ’s complaint. The replacement tokens have not yet been transferred; the agreement depends on a court order.

Forfeiture case and freeze request

The DOJ’s complaint asks the court to forfeit the property to the United States. In a separate motion, prosecutors also asked a federal judge to keep the assets restrained, saying they were unsure when or whether an earlier temporary restraining order would expire. A restraining order was obtained on Nov. 12, The Block reported.

The DOJ said it has helped freeze, seize or return more than $2.9 billion in cryptocurrency from multiple sources since the theft. It described the forfeiture action as part of its work to assist Binance in recovering more than $6 billion in stolen assets.

The next step is for the court to consider the forfeiture case and prosecutors’ request to maintain the restraint. The filing does not establish a final forfeiture, confirm when the court will rule, or say when Circle would issue replacement USDC.
