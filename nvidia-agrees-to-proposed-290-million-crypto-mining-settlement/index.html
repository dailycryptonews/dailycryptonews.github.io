Nvidia agrees to proposed $290 million crypto mining settlement

Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang agreed to a proposed $290 million cash settlement in a shareholder case over crypto mining disclosures; a judge must approve it.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 28, 2026 · 12:26 UTC·2 min read

Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang agreed to a proposed $290 million cash settlement in a shareholder case over crypto mining disclosures, according to The Block’s report. Plaintiffs filed the agreement and asked for preliminary approval on Nov. 24 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California; the deal still needs a judge’s approval.

What the $290 million deal covers

The proposed cash settlement resolves claims that Nvidia and Huang misled investors about how much cryptocurrency mining contributed to gaming GPU sales in 2017 and 2018. The case update from plaintiffs’ counsel says the certified class covers people and entities that bought or acquired Nvidia common stock from Aug. 10, 2017, through Nov. 15, 2018.

The parties accepted a mediator’s recommendation for the $290 million amount on Sept. 24, after mediation on Dec. 3, 2025, and further negotiations, plaintiffs’ counsel says. Nvidia’s insurers are funding most of the settlement, The Block reported. Plaintiffs’ lawyers are seeking up to $62 million in fees from the fund, also according to The Block.

The allegations concern Nvidia’s disclosures about mining demand and the risk that a downturn could affect gaming GPU sales. Plaintiffs claimed the disclosures contributed to a 28.5% share-price drop over two trading sessions in November 2018, The Block reported; that figure is an allegation in the case, not a court finding.

Court approval remains outstanding

The case began in 2018. The district court dismissed it in 2021, but the Ninth Circuit revived claims in 2023. In March 2026, the district court certified the class, and the Ninth Circuit denied Nvidia’s request to appeal that certification in May, according to plaintiffs’ counsel.

The court must still decide whether to grant preliminary approval and later approve the settlement. The proposed agreement does not end the case until that process is complete.
