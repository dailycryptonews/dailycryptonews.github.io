How much collateral is needed for a crypto loan? Estimate yours

How much collateral is needed for a crypto loan? Divide the loan by its LTV for the minimum value, then add a buffer for price moves and growing debt.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 25, 2026 · 13:38 UTC·2 min read

SparkLend borrowers can estimate the minimum collateral value by dividing the loan amount by the collateral asset’s LTV, then supply more to create a buffer.

Say you want to borrow $750 in DAI against WETH, and the applicable LTV is 75%. The minimum collateral value is $1,000: $750 divided by 0.75. That is the borrowing limit, before any safety buffer.

On SparkLend, LTV and liquidation threshold depend on the asset and market. Those parameters affect whether the protocol fits your plans; when SparkLend suits your borrowing needs is worth considering before choosing where to borrow.

How much collateral should you supply?

To borrow below the limit, supply collateral worth more than $1,000 for the $750 loan in this hypothetical case. At $2,000 of collateral value, the same $750 debt uses half of the $1,500 borrowing limit implied by a 75% LTV.

That extra collateral gives the position room to absorb a fall in WETH’s price or an increase in debt. Interest can increase outstanding debt over time, while a lower collateral value reduces the value supporting the loan.

The calculation uses USD values, so the amount of WETH needed changes with its market price. The $1,000 figure is only an estimate under the stated hypothetical LTV; actual asset parameters can vary.

How does collateral affect liquidation risk?

Liquidation risk depends on the health factor, which reflects collateral value, liquidation threshold and outstanding debt. Risk rises as the health factor approaches 1.

At a given LTV, borrowing a smaller share of the limit raises the health factor. Approximately, health factor equals (liquidation threshold ÷ LTV) ÷ the share of the limit borrowed. Borrowing half the limit therefore gives about twice the liquidation-threshold-to-LTV ratio.

For the $750 loan at a hypothetical 75% LTV, supplying $2,000 of collateral means borrowing half the $1,500 limit. Its approximate health factor would be 2 × (liquidation threshold ÷ 75%); the exact value depends on the applicable liquidation threshold and current collateral value and debt.

Use loan amount ÷ LTV to find the minimum collateral value, then choose a larger amount based on the price volatility you can tolerate. The LTV sets the borrowing ceiling; the liquidation threshold and changing debt determine how much room remains before liquidation risk rises.
