Farming APR explained: rewards, swap fees and the trade-offs

Farming APR annualizes estimated token rewards and swap fees; realized returns change with pool volume, liquidity, token prices and reward schedules.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 29, 2026 · 21:50 UTC·3 min read

Farming APR estimates a liquidity provider’s yearly return from trading fees and token incentives, expressed without assuming that earnings are reinvested. The figure is a snapshot, not a promised yield: trading activity, pool size, reward rates and token prices can all change it. For a fuller look at the wallet actions and costs around SushiSwap, see sushiswap.

How is farming APR calculated?

Farming APR annualizes the value of expected rewards against the capital deposited. A basic estimate is annual reward value divided by the position’s value, multiplied by 100; a dashboard may combine incentive tokens with projected swap-fee income.

For example, if a position worth $1,000 is estimated to earn $100 over a year, its simple APR is 10%. The estimate assumes the reward pace and token prices hold steady. A reward token falling in price can cut the return in dollar terms even if the number of tokens earned stays the same.

APR also differs from APY. APY includes compounding: earning rewards and reinvesting them can raise the effective return, but only if the extra transactions and their costs do not outweigh the gains.

How do swap fees reach liquidity providers?

A swap fee is charged when a trader exchanges tokens through a pool. The pool’s rules determine the fee rate and how much goes to liquidity providers; eligible providers receive a share based on their contribution and, in some pools, whether their liquidity is active at the current price.

Fee income depends on trading volume, the pool’s fee rate and the provider’s share of active liquidity. More volume can mean more fees, but a high quoted APR may reflect a short burst of trading that does not continue. Some protocols also direct part of a fee to the protocol, so the full trader charge may not accrue to providers.

What should you compare before farming?

Compare the source and stability of each part of the quoted APR. A fee-driven return rises and falls with trading; an incentive-driven return can shrink when a program reduces rewards or the reward token loses value.

  • Check whether the estimate includes trading fees, incentive tokens or both.
  • Look at the reward schedule and whether rewards are paid in a volatile token.
  • Account for deposit, withdrawal and claim transaction costs.
  • Consider how changes in the two token prices affect the value of your position.

Providing liquidity can also leave a depositor with a different mix of tokens than holding them separately, especially when their market prices diverge. That trade-off can outweigh fees and incentives. The practical choice is to treat APR as a comparison tool, inspect its components and reassess when pool activity or rewards change; future returns remain unconfirmed.
