Solana’s Alpenglow upgrade moves to public testnet

Solana lists Alpenglow live on testnet after Anza recommended Agave 4.3 for mainnet validators, moving its 150ms finality plan into wider testing.

Token Hub EditorialSep 24, 20262 min read

Solana’s Alpenglow consensus upgrade is live on testnet, advancing a change that targets transaction finality in about 150 milliseconds, down from 12.8 seconds, according to the Solana Foundation’s upgrade page. The page lists the testnet activation as live in epoch 1042 and mainnet as not activated.

Anza recommended Agave 4.3 for general adoption by mainnet validators on Sept. 21, according to its release schedule. The software carries Alpenglow’s first phase, Votor. The change is still being tested before any mainnet activation.

What changes in Solana’s consensus system?

Alpenglow replaces TowerBFT, Solana’s current consensus system, with Votor, according to the Foundation. TowerBFT records validator votes as transactions and finalizes a block after votes accumulate across 32 slots; Votor has validators send votes directly to one another and combine them into certificates.

Under Votor, a block can become final after one or two rounds of voting. The Foundation describes 150 milliseconds as a target based on testing, not a fixed time. Actual finality can vary with the validator stake distribution and how many voting rounds are needed.

The upgrade changes how validators agree on blocks, while leaving transaction execution, programs and fees unchanged, the Foundation says. A later phase, Rotor, is planned to replace Turbine, Solana’s block propagation system; it is not yet scheduled.

What does the testnet milestone confirm?

The testnet activation puts Alpenglow on Solana’s established public test network, where developers and validators can exercise the upgrade before mainnet. The Foundation says the testnet status is live; CoinDesk also reported the move to testnet and said participating validators need Agave 4.3.

Anza’s schedule records a staged rollout of Agave 4.3: it asked mainnet operators representing 10% and then 25% of stake to volunteer for the upgrade before recommending general adoption on Sept. 21. That software rollout does not itself activate Alpenglow on mainnet.

When could Alpenglow reach mainnet?

Solana’s upgrade page lists mainnet as not activated. Anza’s schedule marks Sept. 28 as a tentative date to resume feature activation on mainnet, while CoinDesk reported that the date is not a confirmed Alpenglow launch.

The next step is further testing and feature activation across Solana’s networks. The Foundation says each cluster switches at its own slot, so a schedule date alone does not establish that Alpenglow is active; its current status page lists testnet as live and mainnet as not activated.
