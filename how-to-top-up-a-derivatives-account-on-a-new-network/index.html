How to Top Up a Derivatives Account on a New Network

The Blockheight Editors·Sep 30, 2026·3 min read

To top up a derivatives account on an unfamiliar network, check what deposits it accepts, move the right asset to that network, then send it to your account. For example, if your USDC is on Ethereum but the account accepts USDC on Arbitrum, you need to bridge funds before depositing.

A cross-chain bridge moves value between blockchains, sometimes by swapping tokens along the way. Bungee Bridge is one example: it compares routes across bridges and decentralized exchanges, which are trading services run by software. The Bungee Bridge aggregator is relevant when you need to find a route to a deposit network. Bungee Bridge is built by Socket.

What should you check before moving funds?

First, find the exact deposit requirements in your derivatives account. A token with the same name can exist on several networks, and sending it on the wrong one may delay recovery or make funds inaccessible.

  • Network: the exact chain the account accepts, such as Arbitrum or Ethereum.
  • Token: the asset and, when shown, its contract address. A contract address identifies a token on one network.
  • Deposit address: copy it carefully. Check whether the platform also requires a memo or tag, an extra identifier used to credit your account.
  • Minimum and crediting rules: check the minimum deposit and how many confirmations the platform waits for before crediting funds.

Ethereum.org explains that bridges can use different designs and add their own trust assumptions. That means a route’s destination token, bridge method, and expected delivery matter as much as its quoted price.

How do you choose and complete a route?

Compare the available methods before sending the full amount. A direct withdrawal from an exchange may be simpler if it supports the destination network and exact token; bridging is useful when your funds are elsewhere.

  1. Confirm the destination first. Open the derivatives platform’s deposit details and verify the network, token, address, minimum, and any memo. This sets the target for every route you compare.
  2. Check your starting balance and gas. Gas is the network fee paid to process a transaction. You may need the source chain’s gas token for the bridge transaction, and the destination chain’s gas token for later actions. Keep enough aside; don’t plan to send your entire balance.
  3. Compare routes for the same result. Use the same destination network and token when comparing estimated received amounts, route steps, and delivery times. A route may swap your asset before or after bridging. Check whether its estimate accounts for both the swap and network fees.
  4. Review any token approval. Some routes ask you to approve a token allowance, which gives a contract permission to use a specified amount. Read the wallet prompt and approve only the amount needed for the transfer.
  5. Send a modest test deposit when practical. For example, if moving 100 USDC from Ethereum to an Arbitrum deposit address, first send a smaller amount that clears the platform minimum. Confirm it appears in the derivatives balance before sending the rest.
  6. Track each stage. A source-chain transaction can confirm before the destination transfer finishes. Check the transaction record on both networks; once the token reaches your deposit address, the platform may still need its own confirmations before crediting it.

Arbitrum’s documentation describes deposits as messages from a parent chain to a child chain. That helps explain why “sent” on Ethereum does not always mean “available” in an Arbitrum account right away. If the transfer does not arrive as expected, check the transaction status and the platform’s deposit rules before sending again.

What should decide your choice?

Choose the route that delivers the exact deposit asset with enough left over for fees, at a time and cost you accept. A low quoted fee is less useful if the route delivers a token or network the account cannot credit.

In short: verify the deposit details, compare ways to reach that exact destination, preserve gas, and confirm a small transfer when practical. Then send the remaining funds and wait for both network delivery and account crediting.
