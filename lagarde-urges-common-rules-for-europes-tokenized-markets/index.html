Lagarde urges common rules for Europe’s tokenized markets

Christine Lagarde says Europe needs common rules as tokenized markets grow, warning fragmented platforms could block settlement and weaken a single market.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 21, 2026 · 10:07 UTC·2 min read

ECB President Christine Lagarde warned on Nov. 20 that Europe’s tokenized asset market could split into platforms unable to settle trades with one another, and said lawmakers need to establish common rules. In her Frankfurt European Banking Congress speech, she put the global market at about €23 billion in March, five times its size a year earlier.

Lagarde said blockchain lets securities be issued, traded and settled on a single platform. She described tokenization as an opportunity to build an integrated European digital asset market, but warned that different standards and practices could take hold as the market grows. Once they diverge, she said, bringing them back together is difficult.

The ECB president pointed to two Eurosystem initiatives. Pontes, launched in September, allows tokenized transactions to settle in central bank money on Eurosystem infrastructure and under European rules. Lagarde said it gives platforms access to a common settlement asset, so liquidity does not have to be divided among them.

Earlier in November, the European Stability Mechanism settled a €1 billion digitally native bond on Pontes, according to the Cointelegraph report on Lagarde’s warning. The ECB is also working with market participants through Appia on an integrated market, including common standards.

Lagarde said infrastructure alone would not be enough: common rules are needed, and legislators must provide them. The Cointelegraph report said Appia’s blueprint is planned for 2028. Lagarde’s speech gave no timetable for legislation, leaving open when lawmakers might act.
