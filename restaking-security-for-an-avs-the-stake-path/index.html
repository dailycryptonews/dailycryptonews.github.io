Restaking Security for an AVS: The Stake Path

Restaking secures an AVS by putting delegated ETH or liquid staking tokens at risk under service rules, giving operators an economic reason to do the work correctly.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 18, 2026 · 01:57 UTC·2 min read

Restaking secures an AVS by assigning staked ETH or liquid staking tokens to operators whose work follows that service’s rules. The possibility of losing assigned stake gives operators an economic reason to perform their tasks correctly.

On EigenLayer, a restaker delegates eligible stake to an operator. Delegation gives the operator validation weight for the service; it does not give the operator control of the holder’s tokens.

That is the economic-security link in eigen layer restaking. For context on the risk behind that incentive, read cryptoeconomic security and ETH risk; it explains how potential losses can back service rules.

How does delegated stake become an AVS’s security?

An AVS uses operators to perform tasks specific to its service. An operator opts into an AVS and may check or attest to service data, depending on what that AVS requires.

The key detail is allocation: an operator’s full delegated balance does not automatically back every AVS it serves. Only the stake assigned to that AVS’s Operator Set is the stake relevant to its security rules.

That assigned amount determines the economic backing available for penalties. Before treating an operator’s balance as protection, a restaker should check which AVS Operator Sets the stake is allocated to and what rules apply there.

When can an AVS penalize an operator’s stake?

An AVS can put allocated stake at risk when its rules define a fault and specify how evidence can lead to slashing. Say an AVS requires operators to attest to data, and its rules define conflicting attestations as a provable fault.

If evidence shows an operator made conflicting attestations, the AVS’s process can trigger a slash against stake allocated to that service. The penalty creates a cost for breaking the rule; it does not guarantee that every fault is detected or that every penalty will be enforceable.

This is economic security for the AVS: delegated stake backs the operator’s obligations, and a defined violation can put some of that stake at risk. It is separate from Ethereum consensus, which secures Ethereum’s own network; restaking adds service-specific obligations on top.

In practice, follow the chain: a restaker delegates eligible ETH or liquid staking tokens, an operator opts into an AVS and does its work, and the AVS’s rules determine which allocated stake can be penalized. The security depends on the amount assigned to that AVS and on enforceable rules and penalties.
