Bitcoin Slides Below $59,000, Then Rebounds From $55,130

Bitcoin fell below $59,000 on Nov. 20 and later hit $55,130 as stock losses and rate fears deepened selling; it rebounded above $61,000 Monday.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 23, 2026 · 13:41 UTC·2 min read

Bitcoin fell below $59,000 on Friday, Nov. 20, and dropped to $55,130 early Saturday before rebounding to about $61,500 Monday, according to CoinDesk’s market coverage. The weekend low was the cryptocurrency’s weakest level since September 2024.

Reuters reported Bitcoin was down 3.45% at $59,381 when it fell below $60,000 Friday, its first move under that mark since July. Ether was down 5.23% at $1,648.91 at the time.

What drove Bitcoin’s latest drop?

The selloff crossed crypto, technology shares and gold. CoinDesk reported that the Nasdaq fell 3.8% Friday, while the semiconductor ETF SOXX lost 8%; gold fell about 2%. The report linked the pressure to worries over AI company valuations and spending, alongside rising borrowing costs.

Maxime Seiler, co-founder and CEO of trading firm STS Digital, described a broad risk-off move and said cash offered investors a place to wait. Scott Freeman, a market strategist at Delphi Digital, told CoinDesk that weak liquidity left markets vulnerable when investors sold, and warned that further Federal Reserve rate increases could add pressure.

How much did forced selling add to the move?

CoinDesk, citing CoinGlass, reported that roughly $1.5 billion in crypto derivatives positions were liquidated over 24 hours. Those automatic closures occur when traders’ collateral no longer covers their leveraged positions, adding selling as prices fall.

The drop also erased Bitcoin’s August-September rebound to $87,000 and brought the price within about 2% of the $57,700 low recorded in June and July, CoinDesk reported Friday. The later move to $55,130 took it below that level.

What will show whether the rebound holds?

Bitcoin was trading near $61,500 Monday as U.S. stock futures rose, but CoinDesk said the reopening of Wall Street would test whether the weekend rebound could hold. The outlet reported nearly $3 billion in November outflows from spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds; a return to inflows would signal renewed demand.

Whether the rebound continues, and whether Bitcoin can sustain a move back above $62,000, remained unconfirmed in Monday’s report.
