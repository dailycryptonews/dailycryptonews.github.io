How do liquidity providers earn money: bridge fees explained

Liquidity providers earn money from crypto bridges mainly through transfer fees, while token incentives can add income; pool volume and deposit size shape each provider’s share.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 28, 2026 · 07:18 UTC·3 min read

Bridge liquidity providers earn mainly a share of user transfer fees, with token incentives adding income on some networks; what each provider receives depends on routed volume and pool design.

Jumper bridge is a cross-chain bridge aggregator that finds routes through supported bridges and swaps to move assets between networks. For more on how routing and swaps fit into a transfer, Jumper bridge covers that part of the process.

Transfer fees pay providers who supply pooled liquidity

Users pay to move assets using liquidity available on the destination chain, and participating providers receive a share of the fees. Across LPs deposit into the HubPool on Ethereum and earn fees from user transfers, making this route relevant to providers who want exposure to bridge transfer activity.

Volume through the pool drives fee income; pool size affects how that income is divided. If transfer volume and fees stay the same while deposits double, the fee income per deposited dollar roughly halves. A deeper pool can support more transfers, but it needs more volume to maintain the same fee share per unit of capital.

Hop swap fees suit providers willing to supply both sides of a market

Hop’s AMMs pair hTokens with canonical tokens and pay LPs swap fees on each conversion. A transfer that uses a swap can therefore generate fees at the conversion step; this suits providers comfortable holding both assets in the pool.

Those fees depend on how often users trade through the pool. The amount of liquidity alone does not tell a provider how much fee income the swaps will produce.

Token incentives can add a separate payment

Some bridges distribute tokens to attract liquidity, so providers may receive incentives alongside user-paid fees. Stargate is one example of a bridge with token incentives; this route suits providers who are willing to assess a token reward as a separate part of the arrangement.

Incentive income follows the program’s rules and token distribution, while transfer-fee income follows usage. Keep the two sources distinct when reviewing what a pool paid.

Circle CCTP has no LP pool to earn from

Circle CCTP burns USDC on one network and mints USDC on another, so it has no LP pool. It is useful context when comparing bridge designs: not every cross-chain transfer pays liquidity providers.

Questions to check before providing liquidity

What is impermanent loss on a bridge pool?

It is the difference between the value of your pool share and the value of holding the deposited tokens separately, caused by changes in their relative prices. Before depositing, compare the pool’s assets and consider how price changes could affect the amount of each token you can withdraw.

How are LP withdrawals handled when a pool is imbalanced?

A withdrawal returns your share of the pool’s available assets, which may not match your original deposit amounts if trading changed the balance. Check the pool’s withdrawal terms and current asset mix before requesting funds.

Do relayers and LPs share fees?

It depends on the bridge’s fee design. Check the protocol’s fee breakdown to see which amounts go to liquidity providers, relayers, or other participants.

Are bridge LP rewards taxable?

Tax treatment depends on your jurisdiction and the type of reward or transaction. In the UK, HMRC guidance says tokens received from liquidity pool arrangements may count as taxable income in some circumstances. Keep records of deposits, withdrawals, fees, and rewards, then check the rules where you file.
