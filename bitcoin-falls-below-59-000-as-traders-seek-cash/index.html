Bitcoin falls below $59,000 as traders seek cash

Bitcoin fell below $59,000 on Nov. 20, nearing its summer low as strategists cited thin liquidity and Fed rate fears; a market bottom remains unconfirmed.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 22, 2026 · 13:37 UTC·2 min read

Bitcoin fell below $59,000 on Friday, Nov. 20, coming within roughly 2% of its $57,700 June-July low, according to CoinDesk’s market report. The move erased Bitcoin’s August-September rebound to $87,000, the report said.

Why did Bitcoin and other assets sell off?

CoinDesk reported that selling spread across crypto, technology stocks and gold. Ether fell 7%, while XRP, Solana and BNB lost 5% to 6%; about $1.5 billion in crypto derivatives positions were liquidated over 24 hours, according to CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk.

Maxime Seiler, co-founder and CEO of trading firm STS Digital, told CoinDesk that cash was a place to hide during what he described as a broad risk-off move. Delphi Digital strategist Scott Freeman attributed the pressure to thin liquidity and said further Federal Reserve rate increases could deepen the selloff.

What are traders watching at the Fed?

Federal Reserve officials have discussed raising rates to counter persistent inflation. The Associated Press reported on the Fed minutes and officials’ comments that some policymakers supported a rate increase, while Governor Christopher Waller backed a half-point increase at the December meeting. New York Fed President John Williams said he did not think the situation called for “dramatic action,” AP reported.

Higher rates can make cash and bonds more attractive relative to assets that pay no yield, but the reports do not establish that Fed expectations alone caused Bitcoin’s drop. CoinDesk linked the move to a wider selloff and liquidity concerns, while reporting that Freeman saw rate hikes as a risk to markets.

Has Bitcoin found a bottom?

Not yet confirmed. CoinDesk reported that Delphi Digital remained underweight equities and crypto after the decline. Bitcoin’s proximity to its summer low puts that level in focus, but whether it holds—and what the Fed will do in December—remains uncertain.
