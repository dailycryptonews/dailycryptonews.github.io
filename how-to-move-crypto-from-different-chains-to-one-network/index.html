How to Move Crypto From Different Chains to One Network

How to move crypto from different chains to one network: choose a destination token, compare supported routes, and transfer each source balance in turn.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 20, 2026 · 06:47 UTC·2 min read

Jumper bridge can route crypto from several supported chains to one network, where each completed transfer becomes a balance on that destination chain. Bridge routes move assets between chains, sometimes with a token swap along the way.

Each chain keeps its own balances: Ethereum and Arbitrum, for instance, are separate networks. Moving both balances to Base takes two transfers; it does not turn the original balances into one cross-chain balance. For the separate goal of Jumper bridge transfers into one wallet, the wallet is the endpoint; here, Jumper bridge routes each transfer to one network.

How do you move balances from several chains?

Start by choosing a supported destination network and the token you want to hold there. You also need access to the wallet holding each source balance and enough gas on each source chain to submit its transfer.

For each balance, select its source chain and token, then set the destination chain and token. Request a route, compare the quoted amount you would receive, and review the route details, estimated time and fees before choosing.

Route options depend on the selected chains and tokens. LI.FI brings together bridge and swap routes, including routes from providers such as Across Protocol and Stargate Finance. The available providers and route details can vary with your selections.

After choosing a route, approve or sign the source-chain transaction in your wallet. Repeat the process for every source balance, then check that each transfer arrived on the destination network.

What does that look like for Ethereum and Arbitrum?

Say you hold the token you want on Ethereum and Arbitrum, and want both on Base. Request one route from Ethereum to Base and another from Arbitrum to Base. Compare the quoted received amounts, route details, estimated times and fees separately, then sign each transaction on its own source chain.

When both transfers complete, the token balances are on Base. The Ethereum and Arbitrum balances were moved in separate transactions; the result is a consolidated balance on Base, not a shared balance spanning all three networks.

That can make assets available on the network you have chosen to use, while keeping the steps clear: move each source balance, confirm each arrival, and account for the balances together on the destination chain. Choose the destination network and token, then request a route for each source chain.
