How does Aave work for beginners? Aave V3 lending

How does Aave work for beginners? Aave pools pay suppliers in aTokens; borrowers post collateral, pay variable interest and risk liquidation below health factor 1.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 28, 2026 · 02:04 UTC·2 min read

Aave V3 lets users supply crypto to shared pools for interest or borrow from those pools against collateral, with variable-rate debt secured by overcollateralization.

Supply first, then track the position

Aave pools let suppliers make assets available to borrowers and earn interest as those assets are borrowed. Aave Docs says the Aave Interface walks users through connecting a wallet, selecting a network and asset, approving the token, then confirming the supply transaction.

After supplying, you receive aTokens that represent your share of the pool. Aave Docs explains that their wallet balance rises as supply interest accrues; the rate changes with pool use. The Aave V3 Technical Paper describes the underlying aToken and interest-rate mechanics.

Aave staking is a separate way to support protocol risk management and earn rewards. Umbrella lets users stake eligible assets for that purpose; Aave staking via Umbrella is distinct from supplying assets to earn pool interest.

Supplied assets can also serve as collateral when the market allows it. Check the asset’s settings before borrowing: collateral status and risk parameters vary by asset and network.

Borrowing uses variable rates and liquidation limits

Aave V3 new borrows use variable rates; stable-rate borrowing is disabled in V3 markets, even though older guides may still show a choice. The rate changes with pool utilization: it rises as more of an asset is borrowed, then increases faster above the market’s optimal utilization point, the kink in the rate curve. So the rate can change while debt is open.

LTV sets the maximum borrowing value against collateral. The liquidation threshold is a separate limit: it determines when the position becomes eligible for liquidation. Aave Docs defines health factor as collateral value weighted by liquidation thresholds divided by debt value; below 1, the position can be liquidated.

Say you supply $10,000 of ETH and borrow $5,000 of USDC. The market’s LTV must permit that loan. If ETH falls until your health factor reaches 1, a liquidator can repay part of your USDC debt and take ETH collateral plus a liquidation bonus. Repaying debt or adding collateral can improve the health factor; watch it as prices and variable interest change.
