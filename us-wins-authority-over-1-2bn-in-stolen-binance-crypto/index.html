US wins authority over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain $1.2bn of crypto stolen from Binance, part of $2.7bn frozen as recovery efforts continue.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 16, 2026 · 20:18 UTC·2 min read

The US Justice Department said on Nov. 12 that a court had authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The Justice Department announcement said the funds are part of more than $2.7 billion frozen by US authorities and private-sector partners.

The department said more than $6 billion in virtual assets was stolen from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The FBI attributes the theft to TraderTraitor, a group it identifies as members of the North Korean military.

How are authorities tracking the Binance funds?

US agencies have worked with virtual-asset service providers to stop the stolen assets moving, according to the Justice Department. The department said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that tracing and recovery work is ongoing.

The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have urged service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the stolen Binance assets, the department said. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of the $2.7 billion frozen so far; it does not mean that all frozen assets have been forfeited or returned.

What happens before the funds can be returned?

Forfeiture claims typically go before a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds, the Associated Press reported. The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets remain underway.

The department has not said how much of the court-authorized amount has been formally seized, or when any assets might be returned to Binance or affected customers. The next steps depend on continuing recovery work and the forfeiture process.
