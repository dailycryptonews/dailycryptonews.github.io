How to Recover Tokens Sent on the Wrong Network

Sent tokens on the wrong network? Recover them yourself when you control the receiving wallet; contact the exchange or contract owner when they control the address.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 28, 2026 · 03:58 UTC·3 min read

A confirmed EVM transfer with tokens missing at the destination calls for a trace before any recovery attempt: use the transaction hash to find the receiving address on the chain that processed the transfer. Recover the tokens yourself if you control that address’s key; contact the exchange if it belongs to one, or the project if it is a contract. The visible balance and address on that chain confirm where the tokens landed.

Where did the transfer land?

The transaction hash on the sending chain’s explorer shows the transfer’s destination and status. Gather these details before choosing a recovery route:

  • Transaction hash: it identifies the confirmed transfer.
  • Chain used: the explorer must match the chain that processed it, such as Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon, Arbitrum or Base.
  • Destination address and token: they show which account or contract received the funds.

A confirmed transfer can reach the intended address on a different EVM chain from the one you expected. A self-custody EVM wallet uses the same address across EVM chains, so an absent balance in the wallet’s current network view does not by itself mean the tokens are lost.

Who controls the receiving address?

The receiving address’s controller determines the route: use the key if you hold it, contact the exchange if it is an exchange deposit address, and contact the project if it is a contract.

Your own wallet

The tell-tale sign is that the destination address is yours and shows the token on the chain actually used. Add that network in your wallet, get a small amount of its gas token, then swap or bridge the tokens back to the intended chain. Confirm the token and network before moving the funds again.

Once the funds are back where you want them, check the assets you hold before entering a DeFi position. The Beefy app automatically compounds rewards from supported DeFi strategies; when weighing Beefy app ZAP or direct LP deposits, compare the route with the tokens you have ready to deposit.

An exchange deposit address

The tell-tale sign is that the destination address came from your exchange deposit screen. Open a support ticket with the transaction hash, network and token; the exchange controls that address’s key. Many exchanges charge a recovery fee, and some decline recovery, so wait for the exchange’s decision before sending more funds.

A contract address

Paste the destination address into the explorer for the chain that processed the transfer. Contract code marks a contract address: only its owner can return the funds, and only if the contract has a rescue function. A plain account has no contract code; whoever holds its key can move the tokens. Contact the project through a verified channel with the hash and address, and do not share your wallet recovery phrase.

If the address, chain or contract status remains unclear, stop and check the hash and destination with the relevant wallet provider, exchange or project through a verified channel. The deciding fact is control of the receiving address: the party with its key or contract authority is the one that can move the tokens.
