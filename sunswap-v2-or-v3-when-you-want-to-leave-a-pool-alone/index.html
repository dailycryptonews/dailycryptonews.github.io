SunSwap V2 or V3 when you want to leave a pool alone

SunSwap V2 vs V3 pool choice comes down to your pair and attention: V2 suits a hands-off deposit; V3 suits a range you can monitor and adjust.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 27, 2026 · 14:42 UTC·3 min read

SunSwap V2 suits a hands-off liquidity deposit, while SunSwap V3 suits a provider who will watch the pair and adjust a price range. Choose based on how often you can check the position and how volatile the pair is.

  • V2 spreads liquidity across the full price range.
  • V3 concentrates liquidity in a range you choose.
  • V3 can earn more fees per dollar while the price stays in range; outside it, the position stops earning fees.

SunSwap V3 concentrates fees inside your range

V3 puts your liquidity to work only within a chosen price range, so each dollar can earn a larger share of fees while the market price stays inside it. The fee tier is set when the pool is created, and each position is represented by a separate NFT, as SunSwap documentation on SUN.io describes.

That concentration brings a management cost. If the price leaves your range, the position stops earning fees and is held entirely in one token—the one that lost value against its pair. To move the range, you close the position and open another; that takes two transactions, each using TRON Energy.

For a stable pair such as USDT/USDD, a narrow range can fit a provider who is prepared to monitor and rebalance. The TRON exchange SunSwap is where you can act on that pool choice.

Uniswap’s v3 Core whitepaper describes this concentrated-liquidity model and its out-of-range behavior. The trade-off is direct: more fee exposure per dollar in range, but no fee earning from the position while it is out of range.

V2 suits a hands-off deposit

V2 spreads liquidity across the full price range using the constant-product formula x·y=k. The Uniswap v2 Core whitepaper describes that model; SunSwap documentation on SUN.io identifies the same V2 pool mechanics. Because the position covers every price, it remains eligible for fees as swaps happen, though each dollar represents a smaller share of the pool’s liquidity.

V2 positions are fungible LP tokens, rather than separate NFTs for each range. That makes V2 the simpler fit for a holder who checks once a month and does not plan to adjust a position. A volatile pair can suit this hands-off approach better than a V3 range that may be left behind by price moves.

V3’s fee collection also means managing a specific NFT position; V2 tracks the pool share through LP tokens. Choose V2 if you won’t adjust a range; choose V3 if the pair is stable or you’ll rebalance.
