US court authorizes seizure of $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in crypto from Binance’s October theft; recovery efforts continue.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 16, 2026 · 08:43 UTC·2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department said Thursday, November 12, that a court authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in October. The department’s announcement says the theft involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets.

How much of the stolen crypto has been frozen?

The department said U.S. authorities and private sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in assets linked to the theft. More than $1.2 billion of that total is covered by court authorization to seize or restrain the funds, a step that does not itself establish that the assets have been forfeited or returned to victims.

The FBI has attributed the October 29 theft to members of the North Korean military known as TraderTraitor, according to the Justice Department. The department said the actors continued to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges as recovery efforts proceeded.

What does the court authorization mean for Binance and its users?

The action targets stolen assets traced from the breach; the announcement does not say Binance users will receive a payment now. The Associated Press reported on the seizure and the next legal step: the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim, after which a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. It has not announced a final forfeiture ruling or a schedule for returning the seized funds.
