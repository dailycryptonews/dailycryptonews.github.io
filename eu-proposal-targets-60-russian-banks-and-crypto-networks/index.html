EU proposal targets 60 Russian banks and crypto networks

The European Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal would target 60 banks and crypto networks, but needs unanimous approval from all 27 EU states.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 27, 2026 · 13:53 UTC·2 min read

The European Commission proposed on Nov. 23 extending transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and cracking down on crypto and payment networks as part of its 22nd sanctions package. The Associated Press report says the measures also would remove those banks from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the crypto measures would focus on Russia and include third countries that help it evade sanctions. The Commission’s statement on the proposal says a Crypto Sanctions Task Force has been launched to support that work and coordinate with international partners.

  • 60 banks: The proposal extends the transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and removes all 60 from SWIFT.
  • Crypto and payment networks: The Commission says the measures would cover Russia and third countries helping it evade sanctions, but did not name platforms or specify the restrictions in the statement.
  • Task force: The Commission says the new unit will support enforcement of the crypto sanctions.

The announcement is a proposal, not an active restriction. The AP reports that all 27 EU member countries must endorse the package before it can take effect; that process can take weeks, and the measures may change during negotiations.

For crypto businesses and users, the immediate development is a proposed expansion of EU sanctions enforcement to crypto networks and possible facilitators outside Russia. The Commission has not yet specified which services or third-country entities would be covered. The proposal now awaits agreement among EU member states.
