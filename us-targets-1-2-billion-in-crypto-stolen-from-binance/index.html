US targets $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance

The Justice Department says it has court authority to seize or restrain over $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance on Oct. 29, as tracing continues.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 18, 2026 · 17:31 UTC·2 min read

The Justice Department said on Nov. 12 it had court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The department’s announcement says the FBI attributes the theft of more than $6 billion to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor; the Associated Press report describes it as a $6.3 billion theft.

How much has been frozen or targeted?

U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, according to the Justice Department. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that amount and permits the government to seize or restrain it.

The distinction matters: the announcement describes both frozen assets and assets covered by court authority. It does not say that all $1.2 billion has been transferred to government-controlled wallets or returned to Binance or its customers.

How are investigators tracing the stolen crypto?

The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Federal investigators are working with virtual asset service providers to trace and interdict assets, and an FBI alert urged providers to block transactions involving addresses linked to the laundering.

The FBI previously identified the group as North Korean and said it had observed the attackers converting stolen assets primarily into Ether. The Justice Department’s Nov. 12 announcement says recovery work is coordinated by the FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit, with the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation.

What happens to the funds next?

Federal authorities say efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. A forfeiture process would allow a judge to determine who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds, the Associated Press reported.

The department has not announced a final forfeiture or a distribution to affected parties. The amount ultimately recovered, and when any eligible claimants might receive funds, remain unconfirmed.
