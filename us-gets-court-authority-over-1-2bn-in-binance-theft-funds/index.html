US gets court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft funds

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance theft funds, while tracing and recovery efforts continue.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 15, 2026 · 15:51 UTC·2 min read

The US Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, it said Thursday, Nov. 12, as authorities and private firms work to recover the funds. The department’s announcement says the amount is part of a larger pool of stolen assets frozen since the theft.

The FBI identified the alleged hackers as members of the North Korean military known as TraderTraitor. The department said the theft took place on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets.

How much of the stolen crypto is frozen?

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen through joint efforts by the US government and private sector, according to the Justice Department. The $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that frozen amount; the announcement does not say that all of it has been forfeited or returned to Binance.

The Associated Press reported that recovered assets in such cases typically go through a forfeiture claim before a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to them.

What happens to the remaining funds?

US authorities said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing. The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors are still laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and the FBI has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions tied to addresses it identified.

The court authorization gives the government a legal route to pursue the covered assets, while the wider freeze includes funds not specified as part of that authorization. The department has not said when forfeiture proceedings will conclude or how much of the frozen cryptocurrency will ultimately be returned to Binance or other claimants.
