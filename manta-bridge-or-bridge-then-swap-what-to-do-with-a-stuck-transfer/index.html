Manta bridge or bridge then swap: what to do with a stuck transfer

A stuck cross-chain transfer may need a status check, a retry, or a separate swap; choose a route by where the funds are and which asset you need.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 14, 2026 · 10:28 UTC·3 min read

A stuck cross-chain transfer usually calls for checking the original transaction before choosing between a Manta bridge route and a bridge-then-swap sequence. The key difference is whether you need only to move an asset to another chain or also change it into a different asset.

A bridge moves assets between blockchains; a swap exchanges one asset for another. Some services combine those steps into one flow. If your funds are available on the source chain and you want to move and swap in one operation, the Manta Bridge cross-chain bridge-and-swap service lets users move and swap crypto assets across blockchains in a single flow, according to its operator.

Should I bridge or bridge then swap?

Bridge alone when the destination chain and asset already match what you need. Bridge then swap when you need the asset on another chain in a different form, such as moving a token first and exchanging it after arrival.

A combined flow can reduce the number of separate actions you submit, but it also ties the move and exchange together. With separate steps, you can check that the bridged asset arrived before deciding whether to swap; the trade-off is another transaction and another point where processing can take time.

What should I check when a bridge transfer is stuck?

Start with the source-chain transaction hash in the wallet or explorer. If the source transaction is pending or failed, the funds may not have left the original chain; if it is confirmed, check whether the destination transaction or credit has appeared before submitting another transfer.

  • Confirm the source network and destination network match the route you intended.
  • Check the transaction status and hash on the source chain.
  • If the source transaction is confirmed, look for its destination-side result before retrying.
  • Keep the transaction hash and exact asset details available if you need to investigate the transfer.

A confirmed source transaction does not by itself prove that the destination asset has arrived. A bridge may need additional processing on the destination chain, so a second submission made before checking the first can create a separate transfer rather than resolve the original one.

When does a separate swap make more sense?

Use separate steps when you want to verify arrival before choosing the exchange, or when the destination asset is still undecided. Once the asset is on the destination chain, a swap is a distinct action with its own transaction status; the bridge confirmation does not confirm the swap.

For most stuck transfers, first establish whether the source transaction was submitted and confirmed. Then choose a retry, a bridge-only route, or a bridge-and-swap flow based on the funds’ location and the asset you need. The next step depends on the transaction records and the service’s processing; whether a particular transfer has completed remains unconfirmed until its destination result appears.
