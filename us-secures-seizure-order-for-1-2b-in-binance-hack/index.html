US secures seizure order for $1.2B in Binance hack

The Justice Department says a court order covers more than $1.2 billion of assets stolen in Binance’s $6.3 billion hack; tracing and recovery continue.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 18, 2026 · 01:43 UTC·2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department said Nov. 12 it had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in a cyberattack. The department’s announcement says U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in assets linked to the theft so far.

How much of the Binance theft is under U.S. control?

The FBI identified the attackers as North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor, and said they stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The Associated Press reported the theft at about $6.3 billion. The $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization is part of the more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department.

Who is working to recover the stolen assets?

The Justice Department said its National Security Division, Criminal Division and the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia are working with law enforcement partners and virtual asset service providers. The FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit are coordinating recovery efforts with the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation.

Officials said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The FBI has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to move the stolen Binance funds.

What happens to the crypto next?

Investigators are continuing to trace, freeze, seize and seek forfeiture of the assets, the Justice Department said. The court authorization allows the U.S. to seize or restrain the specified funds; it does not establish that all stolen assets have been recovered or returned to Binance.

The AP reported that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department has not announced a final forfeiture decision or a schedule for returning assets.
