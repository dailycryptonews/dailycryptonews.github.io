Gas sponsorship removes one hurdle, not swap costs

Gas sponsorship can remove the need to hold a chain’s native token upfront, but cross-chain swaps still carry network, bridge and execution costs.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 16, 2026 · 19:26 UTC·3 min read

Gas sponsorship lets a service pay a transaction’s network fee for a user, but a cross-chain swap can still include fees on both chains and a cost for moving value between them. The transaction preview and bridge quote show different parts of that bill, so compare the expected amount received with the amount sent.

What does gas sponsorship cover?

Gas sponsorship covers the network fee for a particular transaction; it does not make the network process that transaction for free. A wallet, relayer or app can pay the fee on the user’s behalf, often under conditions such as using a supported wallet or completing a particular swap.

On some account-abstraction systems, a paymaster can fund a smart account’s transaction. A relayer can also submit a transaction and pay its gas. The app’s fee terms determine whether the user pays another way, such as through a service charge or a deduction from the swap proceeds.

For a walkthrough of the steps, see this guide to using QuickSwap for a first cross-chain swap. Check the app’s quote and wallet preview to see which costs are included.

Why can a sponsored cross-chain swap still cost more?

A cross-chain swap involves actions on separate networks. The source-chain transaction may approve or transfer tokens, while the destination side must complete delivery; each network can require its own transaction fee.

The bridge or swap route may also charge a fee or build its cost into the exchange rate. The quoted output can change with liquidity and price movement before execution, so the amount received may differ from an earlier estimate. Sponsorship may cover one gas charge while leaving these other costs in place.

  • Network gas: the fee for processing a transaction on a chain.
  • Bridge or route cost: a fee or rate difference for moving value between chains.
  • Swap price impact: the effect of available liquidity on the exchange rate.
  • Destination gas: a fee that may be needed later to move or use the received tokens.

How should you compare cross-chain swap costs?

Compare the final estimated amount received, not just the sponsored gas line. Check the route, fee breakdown, destination token and whether the destination wallet will need native tokens for a later transaction.

If the app does not show who pays each gas fee or how its sponsorship works, treat the quote as incomplete and review the transaction details before confirming. The exact total depends on the selected route and network conditions; the app’s quote and wallet confirmation are the last checks before execution.
