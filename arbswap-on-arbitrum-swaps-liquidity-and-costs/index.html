Arbswap on Arbitrum: Swaps, Liquidity and Costs

Arbswap lets traders swap tokens and supply liquidity on Arbitrum One and Nova; costs depend on pool fees, price impact and network gas at execution.

The Blockheight Editors·Oct 21, 2026 · 21:38 UTC·3 min read

Arbswap lets users swap tokens and supply liquidity on Arbitrum One and Arbitrum Nova through automated market maker pools, according to Alchemy’s protocol listing. A swap trades against a pool’s token reserves, so the amount received depends on the pool’s price, its liquidity and the size of the trade.

Alchemy describes Arbswap as supporting standard and concentrated liquidity pools, as well as a Symbiosis integration for moving assets between One and Nova in a single transaction. Check the selected network and pair before signing; the arbswap label is the reference point for checking the market under review.

How do Arbswap swaps work?

A swap sends one token into a pool and takes another out at the pool’s current exchange rate. In a standard automated market maker, that rate shifts as the trade changes the balance of the two assets; a larger trade against a shallow pool can therefore receive a worse price.

Concentrated liquidity lets providers place funds within a chosen price range, rather than across every possible price. That can put more liquidity near the current market price, but the pool may have less available for a trade if prices move outside providers’ ranges. Compare the quoted output and price impact before confirming.

What does Arbswap charge for a trade?

The cost has three parts: the pool’s swap fee, network gas and any price impact. Arbswap’s fee can depend on the pool, and the available sources do not establish one current fee for every pair, so check the transaction quote rather than assuming a fixed rate.

Arbitrum’s documentation says ETH pays gas on Arbitrum. Gas changes with transaction conditions, while the pool fee is part of the swap itself; a route that crosses between networks may also involve additional execution steps. The total shown at confirmation is more useful than comparing gas alone.

How does Arbswap liquidity work?

Liquidity providers deposit tokens into a pool so traders can swap against them. In return, providers may earn part of the pool’s trading fees, but earnings depend on trading activity and their share of the pool; they are not a fixed return.

Providing two assets also exposes a provider to changes in their relative prices. If the pool rebalances as prices move, the provider can end up with a different asset mix than if they had simply held the tokens. For most users making an occasional trade, comparing the quote, fee and network gas is simpler than taking on that pool risk.

Which Arbitrum network should you use?

Use the network where the tokens and liquidity you need are available, and confirm the network shown by your wallet before sending funds. Arbswap’s cross-network option is described by Alchemy as a Symbiosis integration; moving between One and Nova is distinct from a swap within a single pool.

Arbitrum’s documentation describes Nova as using AnyTrust data availability, which has a different trust assumption from Arbitrum One’s rollup model. The practical takeaway is to check the asset, pool, network and total transaction cost together. The exact pool fees and route available can change, so confirm those details in the transaction quote before signing.
