World Bank to Explore USDC Payments With Citi and Circle

The World Bank Group will assess regulated dollar stablecoins with Citi and Circle for development fund payments, starting with borrower countries where use is permitted.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 21, 2026 · 22:48 UTC·2 min read

On Nov. 17, the World Bank Group said it will explore using regulated U.S. dollar stablecoins for fund disbursements with Citi and Circle, according to the World Bank’s announcement. The proposed setup would combine Citi’s connections to banking rails with Circle’s infrastructure to mint and settle funds in USDC.

The work will examine whether stablecoins and blockchain payment rails could operate alongside or improve the group’s existing disbursement arrangements, which rely on correspondent banking. The World Bank said the aim is to explore potential gains in payment cost-efficiency, speed and transparency; it did not announce a live payment system or quantify expected savings.

FundsChain already tracks projects in 96 countries

The initiative builds on FundsChain, a World Bank blockchain platform for tracing fund disbursements across investment projects in developing countries. The group said more than 350 projects across 96 countries are onboarded to its digital ledgers, where project participants can see how funds are used.

That existing platform provides a traceability baseline, while the new exploration concerns how funds move and settle. The announcement does not say that USDC will replace correspondent banking or FundsChain, or that any project has begun receiving stablecoin payments.

Initial work depends on local permission

The first phase will focus on an intermediated, interoperable stablecoin payment model and engage borrower countries where digital asset use is supported and permitted, the World Bank said. That makes local rules and approval a condition of participation; the announcement did not name countries or set a rollout schedule.

The group said it may explore working with additional banks over time. It has not confirmed transaction volumes, a launch date, specific cost reductions or a decision to adopt the model at scale. The next step is the exploration with Citi and Circle in eligible borrower countries.
