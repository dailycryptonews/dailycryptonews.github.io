DeFi vaults: how deposits become shares

What is a vault in DeFi in simple terms? It pools one asset, follows a strategy and issues shares that represent a changing claim on the pool over time.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 27, 2026 · 23:53 UTC·2 min read

A DeFi vault pools one asset, deploys it through a defined strategy and gives depositors shares that represent their changing claim on the pool.

How do vault shares track a deposit?

A vault mints shares when you deposit and burns them when you redeem. The share price reflects the pool’s total assets divided by its total share supply: share value = totalAssets / totalSupply.

Say you deposit USDC into a lending vault. The contract issues shares for your portion of the pool, then allocates the USDC according to its strategy. As interest accrues, the pool’s assets grow relative to its shares, so each share can be redeemed for more USDC.

Morpho vaults make the accounting concrete: a curator allocates deposited USDC across lending markets, and shares gain value as interest accrues. For more background, what Morpho vaults are used for adds context on their yield, shares and setup.

Strategies vary by vault. A vault may allocate assets across lending markets or other supported yield sources. Before depositing, read which asset it accepts, where the strategy deploys funds and how shares convert back into that asset.

On Ethereum, ERC-4626 is the standard for tokenized vaults; it was finalized in 2022. The standard defines common ways for vaults to handle deposits, shares and redemptions, which helps applications work with different vaults consistently.

Why does “vault” also mean a loan position?

In DeFi, “vault” can also mean one user’s collateral position backing a loan. That is a different arrangement from a yield vault, which pools deposits from many users and puts them to work under a strategy.

MakerDAO, now Sky, uses “Vault” for a single user’s collateral position. The user locks collateral there against a loan; the position records that user’s collateral and debt. It does not represent a share in a pooled yield strategy.

The distinction is ownership and purpose: yield-vault shares represent a proportional claim on a shared pool, while a CDP vault tracks one user’s collateral against their loan. When you see “vault,” identify the asset held, the strategy or loan attached to it, and whether the interface gives you shares or records a collateral position.
