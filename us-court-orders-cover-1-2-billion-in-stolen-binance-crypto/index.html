US court orders cover $1.2 billion in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department says court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen as tracing and forfeiture work continues.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 18, 2026 · 19:29 UTC·2 min read

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Nov. 12. The amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.

How much was stolen, and who does the FBI blame?

The department said members of the North Korean military group it calls TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The FBI has attributed the theft to the group.

Authorities and virtual asset service providers are tracing and blocking funds as they move through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, the department said. The $1.2 billion figure is the amount covered by court authorization; the broader $2.7 billion figure is the total frozen so far by US and private-sector efforts.

What does the court authorization mean for Binance funds?

The authorization allows authorities to seize or restrain the funds, but it does not mean that the assets have been returned to Binance or to customers. The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The Justice Department’s announcement did not identify the assets’ owners or say how much, if any, had been forfeited or returned. It said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen cryptocurrency are ongoing, as the group continues laundering assets.
