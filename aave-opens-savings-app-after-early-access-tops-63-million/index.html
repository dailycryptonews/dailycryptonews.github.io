Aave opens savings app after early access tops $63 million

Aave Labs opened its savings app to the public after early-access balances exceeded $63 million, with bank links and onchain lending behind the account balance.

The Blockheight Editors·Nov 26, 2026 · 02:35 UTC·2 min read

Aave Labs opened its Aave App to the public on Nov. 25, after it attracted more than $63 million in user balances during an early-access period of less than three months, according to The Block’s report. Aave said anyone can now download the app on iOS and Android without a Ghost Pass.

The app routes customer deposits to lending markets on the Aave Protocol, where borrowers pay interest that becomes users’ earnings. Aave says the app allocates deposits across supported markets and displays them as one balance; accounts are self-custodial and can be opened with an email address or phone number.

At launch, eligible users can connect bank accounts in the United States, excluding New York, the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area. Aave says bank deposits and withdrawals carry no app fees, though banks may charge their own. Users in supported jurisdictions can also deposit USDC, USDT and GHO from eight networks.

The Block reported that Aave Labs is offering verified users a variable 6% base rate for 90 days on balances up to $100,000, with eligible users able to raise the rate to 7.25% through referrals and recurring deposits. The company says rates and eligibility vary by region. The app’s own launch announcement says earnings compound every second and users can withdraw without lockups or penalties.

Aave says it plans to add more deposit, sending and receiving options, additional stablecoin networks and an automatic saving tool in the coming weeks. It has not provided dates for those features. The company also says the app is not a bank and its funds are not insured by a government agency.
